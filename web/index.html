    Contract, job and execution details

    Receipt comparison checks the indexer’s response against our archive. It does not independently verify validator consensus proofs. The OperationConsumed reason comes from the archived wallet execution result; the public receipt records the fee-only outcome.

    Why transaction replay protection isn’t enough

    A restarted worker makes a new transaction. Its hash changes, but the business job stays the same. Semvi consumes that job ID on chain, atomically with payment, so losing a gateway’s local records cannot authorize it again.

    What this guarantees—and its limits

    At most one payment per job ID, per vault. This does not guarantee delivery. Each vault holds 256 permanent IDs. A new vault has a separate namespace. Payment amounts and recipients are public. A refused retry still costs network fees. Signed intents cannot be revoked before expiry; refilling a vault can make outstanding intents payable again. Use short expiry windows.

    Sign once, relay again

    Create a dedicated intent key, sign a job for a fixed vault and recipient, and build a fresh transaction for every retry. Amounts are in microTARI.

    cargo build --examples
target/debug/examples/intent keygen /secure/payer.key
target/debug/examples/intent sign /secure/payer.key \
  mainnet VAULT JOB_HASH RECIPIENT AMOUNT EXPIRY
target/debug/examples/prepare request-spec.json

    Replace the uppercase arguments with your values. A vault must already exist and be funded. Preparation and signing do not submit anything.

    Dry-run before spending

    The loopback wallet client authenticates with a local cookie. By default, it only simulates a transaction. Submission requires an explicit flag and network approval.
